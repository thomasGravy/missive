It is too long to stay fully focused and too short to feel like a real session. Forty minutes, for me, is the sweet spot.

How it works

  1. Pick one task small enough to make visible progress on.
  2. Work on it for forty minutes, phone in another room.
  3. Stop, even mid-sentence. Write one line about where to pick up.

The last step is the important one. Stopping mid-sentence sounds frustrating, but it makes starting the next block almost effortless. Hemingway said something similar about ending the day's writing while you still know what comes next.

For years I told myself I would start a newsletter "when I had something to say." It turns out that is the wrong way round. You find out what you have to say by writing it down, week after week, for someone in particular.

So this is a letter. It will arrive on Sunday mornings. It will be about attention, about craft, about the slow and often boring work of making things well. Sometimes it will be about type, because that is my job. Mostly it will be about the hours in between.

What to expect

  • One letter a week, rarely longer than a cup of coffee.
  • No growth hacks, no threads, no "ten tools that will change your life."
  • Occasional drawings, often crooked.

If that sounds like your kind of Sunday, I am glad you are here.
